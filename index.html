Ads, trackers, sponsor reads — and the words you'd rather not read.

AdSkip is a free browser extension for Chrome and Firefox. It skips sponsored segments inside videos, blocks advertising and tracking requests across 23 curated blocklists, and can hide words and phrases you never want to see again — with a colour bar, a gap, or by removing them completely.

What AdSkip does

Four jobs, each one switchable on its own. Nothing here needs an account, and every decision is yours — including the colour of the bar that hides a word.

Sponsored segments

Skip sponsor reads inside videos

AdSkip reads the community-run SponsorBlock dataset and jumps past the parts of a video that are really an advert: paid promotions, self-promos, "like and subscribe" reminders, intros, outros, recaps, filler and non-music sections inside music videos.

You choose which categories are skipped and which are only muted. The lookup is private by default: the request carries only the first four characters of a SHA-256 hash of the video id, which is not enough to identify what you are watching.

  • Mark your own segments in the on-page editor and submit them
  • Submitted segments apply to your player immediately, before anyone approves them
  • You keep a private, random user id for attribution — no account, no email
Ads & trackers

Block advertising and tracking requests

Blocking happens in the browser's own network layer (declarativeNetRequest), not by injecting scripts into pages. That means no code from a filter list ever runs in your browser.

23 lists in eight categories, about 20,000 enforced rules plus 25,000+ element-hiding selectors — all inside the package. Nothing is fetched while you browse, so blocking also works offline.

  • Ads & popups, tracking & privacy, malware & scams, annoyances
  • Parental control, per-company packs, local-network guard, core & safety
  • Four one-click presets: Light, Balanced, Strict, Family
In-player ads

Handle ads inside any HTML5 player

On players that show a "Skip ad" button, AdSkip clicks it the moment it appears. For ads you cannot skip, it mutes the audio and fast-forwards until the ad is over — then restores your speed and volume exactly as they were.

The engine only acts while an ad signal is present, so normal playback is never touched. You can turn the whole thing off, or keep the mute but drop the fast-forward.

Words

Hide words you never want to read

Add your own words and phrases and AdSkip hides them on every site — feeds, comments, articles, anywhere text appears. This is not a profanity list you have to accept: it is your list, your rules.

Three display modes, chosen by you:

  • Cover it — a coloured bar over the word. The page never moves, nothing reflows.
  • Leave a gap — the word becomes invisible but keeps its space.
  • Remove it — the word is taken out and the text closes up as if it was never written.

Colour is red, black, yellow or any value you pick. Matching is whole-word, so a short word never fires inside a longer one, and it works with Hindi and other scripts. Inputs, text areas, code blocks and editable regions are skipped, so forms and editors are never damaged.

Control

Block anything the lists miss

Hover and click any element to hide it — on that site or everywhere. Or drag a box with the area tool to hide everything inside it, or paint a mask over exactly that region. Both apply instantly and both can be removed later from settings.

For anything more precise there is a "my filters" box that takes familiar uBlock/Adblock syntax: ||ads.example.com^, example.com##.promo, ##.newsletter-popup.

Per site

Two switches for every site

Paused means AdSkip does nothing on that site at all. Words off keeps ad blocking but leaves your word list alone there — useful on a site where a common word of yours is part of the layout.

Both lists live in Settings → Sites, and both are reachable in one keystroke or from the right-click menu while you are on the page.

Never breaks a site

Sites are never blocked

Only requests inside a page are blocked — never the page itself. AdSkip also refuses to hide the document, and it honours the "leave this site alone" exceptions published by the list authors, plus a built-in allow list for search engines and code CDNs.

Diagnostics

A log you can actually read

When something misbehaves, AdSkip can tell you why: which ruleset was active, whether an ad was detected, how long a skip took, what failed. At most 400 events, stored on your device, exportable as JSON and clearable at any time.

Privacy

No account, no telemetry

There is no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no server that receives your data. Hostnames can be left out of the log entirely. The only network call AdSkip makes is to the community SponsorBlock API.

Guides

Short, practical, in the order most people need them.

01 Install on Firefox — one click

AdSkip on addons.mozilla.org

The signed add-on is published on Mozilla's official store, so it installs like any other Firefox add-on and updates itself.

Add to Firefox Read the listing

Requires Firefox 140 or newer, including Firefox for Android 142+. The install prompt shows the browsing-activity data declaration — that is the SponsorBlock lookup described above.

Prefer the manual route, or on Chrome? Both are below.

02 Install on Chrome, Edge, Brave or Opera

  1. Download the extension package and unzip it into a folder you will keep.
  2. Open chrome://extensions and turn on Developer mode (top right).
  3. Click Load unpacked and select the unzipped folder.
  4. Pin AdSkip to the toolbar from the puzzle-piece menu so the pause switch is one click away.

03 Install on Firefox manually

  1. Temporary: open about:debugging#/runtime/this-firefox, click Load Temporary Add-on and pick the manifest.json inside the unzipped folder. Temporary add-ons disappear when Firefox closes.
  2. Permanent: install from addons.mozilla.org — no temporary step needed. For self-distribution, sign the package with web-ext sign.
  3. Firefox 140 or newer is required, because the add-on declares the browsing-activity data collection that Firefox shows you at install time.

04 First five minutes: pick a preset

Open Settings → Blocklists. Start with Balanced unless you know you want more or less.

05 Hide a word — two ways

  1. Fastest: select the word on any page and press Alt+Shift+W. A bar appears at the bottom; click Block it. It is hidden everywhere from that moment.
  2. Bulk: Settings → Words, paste one word or phrase per line, then pick the mode — cover, gap or remove — and the colour.
  3. If a site needs the words back, add it under Settings → Sites with the action Words off only.
Whole-word matching is on by default: a blocked word will not fire inside a longer word. Phrases work too — write them with normal spaces.

06 Remove an element that the lists miss

  1. Press Alt+Shift+X (or right-click → AdSkip: block an element).
  2. Hover the page: the element under the cursor is outlined. Click to hide it.
  3. Choose this site or all sites. The rule is saved instantly and can be deleted from Settings later.
  4. For a region instead of a single element, press Alt+Shift+A and drag a box.

07 A site looks broken? Pause it, don't uninstall

  1. Press Alt+Shift+P, or right-click → AdSkip: pause on this site.
  2. The site is added to Paused sites and AdSkip does nothing there until you remove it.
  3. If only your word list is the problem, use Words off only from Settings → Sites instead — ads stay blocked.

08 Write your own filter rules

Settings → Blocklists → My filters. One rule per line.

RuleWhat it does

09 Report a bug so it can actually be fixed

  1. Open Settings → Diagnostics and set the level to debug.
  2. Reproduce the problem on the page once.
  3. Come back and press Refresh, then Export JSON.
  4. Send that file to br19shc39@gmail.com or a DM on @osintgram_io, with the site name and what you expected.
The export contains hostnames and technical events only — no page content, no URLs with paths, no cookies, no tokens. You can also turn hostname logging off before exporting.

Questions people actually ask

 

Does it block every ad?

No, and no extension does. Ads served from the same domain as the page, baked into a video stream, or rendered inside a first-party player can slip through — that is what the element picker and the word filter are for. What AdSkip does well is the request layer: ad networks, trackers, malware hosts and annoyances.

Will it break websites?

Less than a bare filter list would. AdSkip never blocks the page itself, only requests inside it; it never hides the document; it ships the list authors' own "unbreak" exceptions; and it carries an allow list for search engines and code CDNs. If something still breaks, the pause switch for that site takes one keystroke.

What data does it collect?

None that leaves your device, except the SponsorBlock lookup described above. There is no analytics, no account and no telemetry endpoint. The optional diagnostics log stays in your browser and is only exported if you press the button. Full detail: the Privacy policy.

Why does the word filter not touch text boxes?

Because hiding text inside a form field would corrupt what you are typing. AdSkip skips inputs, text areas, code blocks and editable regions on purpose. It also skips scripts and styles, so nothing can break the page's own code.

Do the filter lists get updated automatically?

No — lists are bundled at build time and shipped inside the extension, which is why blocking works offline and why no request is made while you browse. Updated lists arrive with a new version of AdSkip.

Can I use it on Firefox for Android?

Yes, the add-on supports Android from Firefox 142 onwards. The settings page is a normal web page, so it works in a mobile tab too.

Is my word list uploaded anywhere?

Never. It is stored in your browser's extension storage like the rest of your settings, and it is included in the settings export only if you export your settings yourself.

Something is wrong with a specific site. What helps most?

An exported diagnostics file plus the site's name. That tells exactly which ruleset was active, whether the element was detected and what failed — far more useful than a description. See guide 08.

About AdSkip

Built by one person, for the web they want to read.

Why it exists

Most blockers either do too little or break things. AdSkip started as a small tool to skip sponsor reads in videos, and grew into something more deliberate: block the advertising and tracking layer, never block the site, and give the person using it real control — including over the words they have to look at.

Everything runs locally. There is no account, no subscription, no "premium tier", no analytics and no server holding your data. The filter lists ship inside the package, so the extension works the same offline as it does online, and nobody can tell which sites you visit.

Everything about data is spelled out in the Privacy policy: what is stored on your device, what leaves it, and what never does.

The word filter is the part people ask about most. It exists because "I don't want to read this" is a legitimate preference — on a feed, in a comment section, in a group chat screenshot. You decide whether a match becomes a coloured bar, an empty gap, or disappears completely.

How it is built

Plain, unminified JavaScript and the browser's own blocking engine — no bundler, no remote code, no obfuscation. Blocklists are converted to declarative rules at build time and validated on every build: no rule may match every URL, no rule may block the page itself, and the total must stay under the browser's static-rule allowance. 177 automated checks guard the parser, the word filter, diagnostics and packaging.
